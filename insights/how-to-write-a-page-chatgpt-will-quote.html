How do you write a page that ChatGPT will quote? The answer-first format

By Sufia Aram, Managing PartnerPublished 24 October 20264 min read
The short answerA page that AI engines quote has five things. The title is the question a buyer asks. The answer is in the first paragraph, in two to four sentences, with a number or a name in it. The headings are the next questions. The paragraphs are short and give facts, not slogans. There is a FAQ at the end. Most Dubai business pages start with a slogan and hide the answer lower down, so the AI finds nothing to quote.

What the engine is doing

When ChatGPT, Perplexity or Gemini answers a question, it has a few seconds to read the pages it found and pull out a sentence or two that answers it. It is not reading your page the way a visitor does. It is scanning for the passage that matches the question, checking whether that passage contains something concrete, and deciding whether to quote it. Pages win or lose in that scan.

So the format below is not a writing style. It is a way of making the matching passage easy to find and worth quoting.

The five features

1. The title is the question. Not "Our Services". Not "Premium Dental Care in Dubai". "How much does a dental implant cost in Dubai?" The engine matches titles to questions first. If a buyer's question is in your title, you are a candidate; if not, you are competing from behind.

2. The answer is in the first paragraph. Two to four sentences, directly under the title, that answer the question as a knowledgeable person would if stopped in the corridor. Include a number, a name or a place: "A single dental implant in Dubai costs between AED 5,000 and AED 12,000 depending on the implant brand and whether a bone graft is needed. At our clinic in Jumeirah the standard implant is AED 6,500 including the crown." That paragraph is what gets quoted. Everything else on the page supports it.

3. The headings are follow-up questions. "What affects the price?" "How long does it take?" "Is it covered by insurance in the UAE?" Each heading is a question the same buyer would ask next, and each section answers it the same way: directly, first sentence.

4. The paragraphs are short and factual. Three or four sentences. No "we pride ourselves", no "world-class", no "solutions". The engines are trained to discount marketing language and favour statements they could verify. Say what the thing is, what it costs, how long it takes, who does it, where.

5. A FAQ block at the end, with schema. Four to six questions with two-sentence answers, marked up with FAQ structured data so the engine can read them as question and answer pairs. These often get quoted on their own.

Before and after

Before. Title: "Real Estate Services". Opening: "At Marina Prime Properties, we are passionate about helping you find your dream home in Dubai. Our dedicated team of experts brings years of experience and a client-first approach to every transaction." Nothing here can be quoted in answer to any question a buyer asks.

After. Title: "How do I rent out my apartment in Dubai Marina, and what does it cost?" Opening: "To rent out an apartment in Dubai Marina you need an Ejari-registered tenancy contract, a DEWA connection in the tenant's name and a landlord permit if the building requires one. A broker in Dubai typically charges the landlord 5% of the annual rent, and a furnished one-bedroom in the Marina rented for AED 95,000 to AED 130,000 a year in 2026." That paragraph answers the question, contains four facts, and names the area. An engine asked "how do I rent out my Marina apartment" has something to lift, and it has a name to attach to it.

Three mistakes that undo it

  • Burying the answer under a hero image and a slogan. If the first text on the page is "Welcome", you have lost the scan.
  • Hedging every fact. "Prices may vary, contact us for a quote." The engine cannot quote a non-answer. Give a range and say what moves it.
  • Writing one page for six questions. One page, one question. If you have six questions, you have six pages, and six chances to be quoted.

How to apply it this week

Take your three most important service pages. For each, write the question a buyer would type, make it the title, and write the two-to-four sentence answer with a number in it. Put that paragraph first. Then turn your existing content into question headings. Add four FAQs. That is a day's work for three pages, and it is the single highest-return change a Dubai business can make for AI visibility.

Frequently asked

What makes an AI engine quote a web page?

A title that matches the question asked, a direct answer in the first paragraph with a concrete fact in it, question-style headings, short factual paragraphs, and a FAQ block with schema.

How long should the answer paragraph be?

Two to four sentences, directly under the title, containing at least one number, name or place. That paragraph is what gets quoted.

Should one page cover several questions?

No. One page per question. Several questions means several pages, each a separate chance to be quoted.

Does marketing language hurt AI visibility?

Yes. Engines discount slogans and superlatives and favour statements they could verify, such as prices, timelines, locations and names.
